# Settings: Apps

Apps are the services Row-Bot can work in, such as Notion, GitHub or a tool that runs on this computer. Your GitHub, Google and X accounts, the Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, Slack and SMS channels, and the tools that use your own key are apps too. **Settings › Apps** is where you find them, connect them, choose what each one may do, change their settings, and turn them off or remove them.

## Where To Find It

Open **Settings**, then choose **Apps** in the **Connections** group, or search for it with **Find a setting**. The command palette's **Connect an app** opens it too, and requests such as "connect google" or "connect telegram" open that app's page. In a chat, **+ › Apps › Find more apps** opens it; see [Apps in a chat](/docs/chat/#apps-in-a-chat).

## Find An App

Search is instant and local: Row-Bot searches catalog copies kept on this computer, and nothing you type leaves it. To ask the live catalogs (ClawHub and Hermes) about the current search, choose **Search online catalogs**.

The page lists:

- **Your apps**: the apps you have added, with anything that needs attention first. Each shows its status: **Ready**, **Off**, **Sign in needed**, **Key needed**, **Finish setup** or **Needs attention**.
- **Featured**, then category chips (Productivity, Developer, Data, Design, Communication, Finance and Local tools), then **All apps**. **Communication** lists the messaging channels.

While the app catalog is first being built, the page says **Preparing the app catalog…** and more apps appear in a moment.

Each app appears once in results; its other ways to connect are on its page. Apps from their own vendors and from Row-Bot come first, then **More from the community**. Listings that look like placeholders, staging copies or duplicates are left out; **Show all results** shows them too.

Each card shows the app's logo, its name and what it does, and who publishes it: **by _Vendor_** with a check when the service's own vendor publishes it, **Built in** for what comes with Row-Bot, the publisher's name, or **Community**. It also says how it connects: **Hosted · Sign-in**, **API key**, **Hosted** or **Runs on this computer**. Package entries from the Hermes catalog say **Checked when you add**, or are marked unavailable when their pinned version has no Agent Plugins manifest (packages made for the Hermes app itself). Logos come only from Row-Bot or its saved copies; browsing never downloads them, and a letter stands in until a logo is ready.

## The App Page

Open an app to see its page (for example `/settings/apps/notion`). The header shows its logo, name, publisher and status, one main action, and a **⋯** menu.

The main action is whatever comes next: **Connect**, **Add**, **Set up**, **Sign in**, **Sign in again**, **Add key**, **Continue setup**, **Check again**, **Turn on**, **Fix**, **Retry**, **Try it** or **Delete saved data**. The **⋯** menu holds **Turn off**, **Check for updates**, **Advanced settings** and **Remove…**; for a built-in way to connect it holds **Open its settings**.

- **Overview**: what it does, things to ask, and where your data goes.
- **Access**: what it may do, grouped as **Looks things up**, **Makes changes** and **Always asks first**, with a count such as "8 of 12 actions on" and **Choose** to change it.
- **Settings**: for a connected app, the account, whether a key is saved (in your system keychain), each setting, and anything it needs on this computer, with **Change settings**. See [Change settings after setup](#change-settings-after-setup). For a built-in way, **Set up** or **Open** opens its own settings.
- **Ways to connect**: when an app can be connected more than one way, every way, the recommended one first. See [Ways to connect](#ways-to-connect).
- **Included**: for a package, the apps and skills it brought. Each of those says **Installed as part of _package_**.
- **Details**: source, version, licence, other catalogs it is listed in, and its identifier.

## Ways To Connect

Some apps can be connected in more than one way: through a server the service hosts, through a server that runs on this computer, or through a way built into Row-Bot. The app's page lists them under **Ways to connect**, the recommended one first and marked **Recommended**. Each way says how it connects (**Built in**, **Hosted · Sign-in**, **Hosted · API key**, **Hosted** or **Runs on this computer**) and who publishes it; the one you are looking at says **This one**. Choose another way to open its page.

For example:

- **GitHub**: GitHub's hosted server (with a token, or with your own OAuth app), then the GitHub account built into Row-Bot, then GitHub's server on this computer.
- **Google**: the Google account built into Row-Bot first.
- **Slack**: Slack's hosted server, then the Slack channel built into Row-Bot.
- **Tavily**: Row-Bot's built-in **Web search** with your Tavily key, then Tavily's hosted server, then its server on this computer.

Each way keeps its own credentials. A GitHub account token and a GitHub server connection, for example, never share a token: connecting one doesn't sign in the other.

## Built-In Ways To Connect

Some ways to connect are part of Row-Bot itself, so their cards and pages say **Built in**:

- the **GitHub**, **Google** and **X** accounts;
- the **Telegram**, **WhatsApp**, **Discord**, **Slack** and **Text messages** (SMS) channels;
- tools that use your own key: **Web search** (a Tavily key) and **Wolfram Alpha**.

A built-in way is set up and changed in its own settings, not with a setup sheet. Its **Set up** (or **Open** once it is set up), and **⋯ › Open its settings**, open those settings for that one account, channel or tool only, with **Back** to the app page:

- an account opens its step-by-step guide, **Check**, **Reconnect** and **Disconnect**, and what Row-Bot may do with it. See [Accounts And Authorisation](/docs/integrations/accounts).
- a channel opens its connect steps, **Start** and **Stop**, pairing and paired accounts. See [Channels](/docs/integrations/channels).
- a key tool opens its switch and its key, which stays in your system keychain.

Old links to the Accounts and Channels settings pages open the matching app, for example **Apps › Google** or **Apps › Telegram**.

## Connect An App

1. Choose the main action. A sheet named after it, such as **Connect Notion**, lists what will happen: where requests go, whether you sign in in your browser or paste a key, anything Row-Bot needs to download, and that apps get turned on. **How Row-Bot keeps this safe** explains the protections.
2. Agree, and Row-Bot does the setup itself, showing each step as it goes.
3. It pauses only when it needs you: to sign in (your browser opens), to add a key or a setting, to check what it will install, to open a program, or to choose what the app can do.
4. Choose access on **Here's what _app_ can do**, and the app is ready.

### What A Setup Can Ask For

- **Add your key** or **Add your settings**: a key or token is kept in your system keychain; a setting such as a workspace, region or folder is saved with the app. **Where do I get this?** opens the service's own page for it. Optional settings can be left empty.
- **Sign in to _app_**: when Row-Bot first connects, it checks whether the service asks you to sign in, so an app whose listing doesn't say can still sign in. Row-Bot signs in through your browser with its own published details, or registers itself with the service. A few services, such as GitHub, accept only your own OAuth app: Row-Bot asks for its client ID and client secret (both kept in the keychain) and shows the callback address to register, `http://127.0.0.1/api/v1/settings/mcp/auth/callback`. If a sign-in expires and can't be renewed, the app says **Sign in needed**.
- **Get ready to run _app_** and **Install what _app_ needs**: an app that runs on this computer may need Node.js or uv, which Row-Bot sets up for its own use, and an npm package, Python package, container image or bundle, which is installed into its own folder, never system-wide. Before anything installs, Row-Bot shows **Exactly what will be installed**: each package's exact version and checksum, checked again when it installs. Install scripts never run. Python apps use Row-Bot's own uv and Python. Container images need Docker Desktop, already installed, and never get access to your folders, ports or network. If what an app would install changes after you agreed, Row-Bot shows it again and waits.
- **Open _program_**: apps that work with a program on this computer, such as Blender, check that it is running. Open it, then choose **Check again**.

Searching and reading an app's page never install or run anything; nothing installs until you agree and choose **Install**.

**Stop** cancels a setup and keeps what was already done. A setup left waiting for more than 30 minutes stops by itself, also keeping what was done. If Row-Bot can't confirm that a change finished, the page shows **Finishing your last change…** with **Retry**; it checks the result and never repeats the change blindly.

## Choose What An App Can Do

| Choice | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Read only** | Looks things up. Cannot change anything. |
| **Ask before changes** (default) | Looks things up and asks you before every change. |
| **Full access** | Makes routine changes without asking. Risky actions still ask. |

**Customise** sets each action on its own: **Use without asking**, **Ask first** or **Off**.

Some apps overlap with what Row-Bot already does, for example Notion with documents or Slack with channels. Their access step notes it, such as "Row-Bot also has its own documents tools", and the choices work as usual. Changes to files and repositories, such as creating a file or a pull request, always ask first.

:::info Some actions always ask
Deleting, sending, paying, publishing, running code and anything Row-Bot can't identify always ask first, whatever you choose. Full access only covers routine changes such as creating or editing. Apps connected before this version keep asking before routine changes until you choose otherwise.
:::

## Change Settings After Setup

A connected app's settings stay editable. Its **Settings** section shows what it is set to now: the account, whether a key is saved, each setting (a switch reads **Yes** or **No**), and where it runs. **Change settings** opens **Settings for _app_** with every setting the app has, optional ones too:

- A saved key is never shown. Its field stays empty and says **Saved in your system keychain. Leave it blank to keep it.** Type a new key only to replace it.
- **Save** keeps your changes, and Row-Bot checks the connection again. If the app now offers actions it didn't have before, Row-Bot asks what they may do first; the rest keep the access you chose.
- An app that was on reconnects with the new settings; one that was off stays off until you turn it on. The page then says **Settings saved.**

A built-in way's settings are changed in its own settings: choose **Open**, or **⋯ › Open its settings**.

## Turn Off Or Remove

Both are in the app's **⋯** menu.

- **Turn off** stops Row-Bot using the app. Its settings and saved keys are kept.
- **Remove…** opens **Remove _app_**. Keys and data are kept unless you switch on **Also delete saved keys and data**. Removing a package removes everything it brought.

A built-in way has neither; use its own settings instead, such as an account's access switches or **Disconnect**, a channel's **Stop**, or a key tool's switch.

## Add From Link Or File

**Add from link or file** adds something that isn't in a catalog. Paste an https link (an app's address, a GitHub repository or a skill page; Row-Bot detects which, and **This is** lets you change it), or choose a .zip or .skill file (up to 20 MB) for skills and packages, or a .mcpb bundle (up to 128 MB).

A bundle is checked as soon as you choose it: Row-Bot reads its manifest, checks that it runs on this computer and, if it is signed, that the signature matches its contents. A bundle with a broken signature, or made for another system, is refused. The settings a bundle asks for become its **Add your settings** step. A key pasted as part of a link is never saved with the address: Row-Bot asks for it as a key instead.

Nothing is downloaded or run until you agree on the item's page.

## Advanced

**Advanced** on the Apps page (`/settings/apps?view=advanced`) holds:

- **Catalogs**: each catalog with its state, such as **Included with Row-Bot**, when it was last updated, **Couldn't update. The last copy is still used.**, or **Not available yet**, with **Update** and **Update all**. **Update catalogs automatically** is off by default; turn it on to update every day, week or month. **Windows connectors** appears only on Windows builds that have them, and **Composio**, a hosted broker, stays off until you turn on **Use Composio** after reading what it means (see [MCP](/docs/integrations/mcp#hosted-brokers-composio)).
- **Apps in chats**: **Use apps** (turn off to stop every app at once; each keeps its settings), **Show app views in chat** (interactive views from apps that have them; on by default), whether chats are offered app tools, and the shared runtimes (Node.js and uv) that local apps use.
- **Your own connection**: **Add a custom connection** opens the connection editor for a service that isn't in a catalog.

An app's **⋯ › Advanced settings** opens the raw settings for that one app only.

## Old Links

Settings no longer has Integrations, MCP, Plugins, Accounts or Channels pages. Old links to them open **Apps**, or **Apps › Advanced** for catalogs and runtimes, or the item they named: an old account or channel link opens that app, such as **Apps › Google** or **Apps › Telegram**, and an old Channels link that named no channel opens the **Communication** apps. Skills are on their own page: see [Settings: Skills](/docs/settings/skills).

## Privacy And Safety

Searching, browsing and opening an app page stay on this computer. A catalog is contacted only when you choose **Search online catalogs**, **Update**, or automatic updates; updating the Hermes catalog also reads each new package's manifest, never its code. Requests to an app go where its page says once you use it. Keys, tokens and OAuth client secrets are kept in your system keychain, never in settings files, logs or what the page shows. Row-Bot's sign-in details are published at `https://row-bot.ai/oauth/client-metadata.json`; they contain no secret and name only this computer as where a sign-in returns. Checking that a program is open looks only at this computer. Apps that run on this computer are not sandboxed, so add only apps you trust.

## Troubleshooting

- **Sign in needed** or **Key needed**: open the app and use the main action. Overview's **Needs you** lists these apps too, with the fix.
- **Install Docker Desktop, then try again**: the app runs as a container image; install Docker Desktop or choose another way to connect on the app's page.
- A program-based app keeps asking to **Check again**: make sure the program and its add-on (for Blender, its MCP add-on) are running on this computer.
- **Needs attention**: open the app and choose **Fix** or **Retry**.
- If an app's tools are missing in chat, check that it is **Ready**, that it is checked in that chat's **+ › Apps**, that **Use apps** is on and **Apps in chats** offers app tools, and that the chat's agent profile allows it.
- A built-in account or channel that won't work: choose **Open** on its app page and use its own **Check**, **Reconnect** or **Start**.
- If a catalog says **Couldn't update**, Row-Bot keeps using the last copy; try **Update** later.
