# Settings: Models

The Models page is what Row-Bot thinks with, and where it comes from: the model for each job, your starred models, and the providers you have connected (see [Settings: Providers](/docs/settings/providers) for connecting each kind).

<Screenshot id="settings-models" alt="Settings: Models in Row-Bot." caption="The Models page lists the defaults, your models, the connected providers and the model catalog." />

## Where To Find It

Open **Settings**, then choose **Models** in the **Models** group, or search for it with **Find a setting**.

## Defaults

Each job has its own picker, using the same grouped, searchable list as the composer's model picker (provider, how it is paid for, and whether it is available):

- **Main model** is the default model for new conversations and background work. A conversation can still choose its own model in the composer. Background work tied to a conversation, such as checking its goal's progress or compacting it, uses that conversation's model; memory extraction and Dream Cycle use the main model. Either way, Row-Bot uses the lowest reasoning effort that model offers, when it knows the model's reasoning choices.
- **Vision** reads images, screenshots and your camera. Leave it as **Same as main model** if your main model can see, or choose another model. Choose a **Camera** here too.
- **Image** makes and edits pictures.
- **Video** makes short clips and animates pictures.

Turn Vision, Image or Video off when you don't want that job done at all.

The Image and Video pickers offer the models each connected provider's own model list has right now, so a new image or video model appears after **Refresh catalog**, without a Row-Bot update. When a provider stops listing the model you chose, usually because it retired it, the picker says so: choose another.

## Your Models

**Your models** lists the models you starred, which every picker lists first. Each says what it can do (chat, sees images, makes images or video). The check mark makes a model the main model; its star takes it out. Star more in a provider's sheet or in the Catalog.

## Connected

**Connected** has a tile for each provider you have connected, grouped by where it runs and how you pay: on this computer, subscriptions and API keys. A tile says how it is paid for and how many models it has, or what needs doing, such as **Reconnect**.

- Choose a tile to open its sheet: its state, its one way to connect (an API key, a sign-in, or Claude Code's steps), its models with a star each, and a button to refresh its model list.
- **Add a provider** lists the rest by how you pay: **Subscription**, **API key** and **Your server** (an OpenAI-compatible server). Choosing one opens its sheet to connect it.
- **Custom endpoints** below lists your own servers, with their address, probe and key.

A link to `/settings/models?provider=<id>` opens that provider's sheet.

## Reading Limit

**Reading limit** sets how much text the main model reads at once. **Automatic** uses what the model supports; **Limit…** sets a smaller number of tokens, useful for local models on smaller computers.

## Limits For Long Work

These limits apply to new runs; work in progress keeps the limits it started with. **Recommended limits** restores the defaults. A run has no step limit to set: it goes on while it makes progress, and after a very long run of steps asks **Keep going?**.

| Limit | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Helper levels** | How deep delegation goes; 1 lets helpers work but not start helpers of their own. |
| **Helpers at a time, per agent** | Delegated agents one agent runs at once; more wait in line. |
| **Helpers at a time, in total** | Delegated agents running across the whole app. |
| **Helper time limit (seconds)** | How long one helper may work; 0 means no limit, and time waiting in line doesn't count. |
| **Goal turn limit** | The turn limit new goals start with; 0, the default, means no limit. Each goal can change it. |

## Catalog

The **Catalog** lists every model your providers offer. Search it and filter by provider and job. Each model has the same **star** as the models on this computer:

- A starred model is in **Your models**, listed first wherever you choose a model: every job's picker and the composer's model picker, for the jobs it can do. It says **In your models**.
- A provider with nothing starred lists all its ready models in the pickers; starring one keeps its list to your stars.

The status line at the top says how many models there are to choose from, and from how many providers. **Refresh catalog** asks your providers what is available now.

## Common Workflow

1. Connect a provider with **Add a provider**, then choose your **Main model**.
2. Choose Vision, Image and Video models if you want those jobs.
3. Star one everyday model and one stronger tool-capable model.
4. Keep the recommended limits unless you have a measured reason to change them.

## What Is Saved

Job models, limits and your starred models are global preferences. A conversation keeps its own model when you choose one in it.

## Privacy And Safety

A hosted model receives the prompts, images or files its job sends it. Choose local models for jobs that should stay on your computer.

## Troubleshooting

- If a picker is empty, connect a provider first.
- If a model is missing, refresh the catalog and check whether its provider is connected.
- If a picker says its provider no longer lists your model, the provider has probably retired it. OpenAI, for example, retired its gpt-image-1 models in October and December 2026; choose one of its current image models.
- If a model shows as unavailable, read its reason; reconnecting its provider usually fixes it.
- If a small local model fails before answering, lower the reading limit or choose a more capable model.
